# Define brand positioning

Turn the actual offer, audience evidence and existing commitments into a positioning decision or a bounded proposal. Reuse accepted strategy; a request to sharpen one promise need not reopen the whole brand.

`validate-product-idea` owns evidence for the next product investment. `define-writing-voice` owns reusable language conventions; `design-brand-identity` owns visual identity. A positioning exercise cannot establish product-market fit, customer preference or a conversion gain.

## Establish the decision and evidence

Inspect the supplied product facts, intended audience, current positioning, customer language and actual alternatives, including doing nothing or using a manual process. Separate observed behavior, customer quotations, the business's claims and hypotheses. Keep private source details in their permitted location; a public brief needs references its audience can see.

Ask concise questions about consequential missing choices such as the intended audience, offer or retained brand commitments, then wait before dependent decisions. Requested options and source inspection can continue. Reuse settled answers and explicitly delegated choices; do not repeat approval for work already authorized. Sparse evidence can support a provisional brief with named assumptions, not invented interviews or a universal brand-strength score.

## Compare plausible positions

Evaluate materially different positions only where the choice is open. For each, identify the audience situation, relevant alternative, difference the business can deliver and evidence that the difference matters. Distinguish a capability from its customer consequence; a technical feature alone does not show customer value.

Check the promise against actual delivery limits and available proof. Mark comparative or outcome claims that lack an appropriate baseline or source; use narrower factual wording or keep them as hypotheses. An empty category or competitor-map quadrant is not evidence of demand. Keep competitor observations dated and separate from judgments about how customers perceive them.

Recommend the position supported by the evidence, explaining the trade-off and the fact that would change the choice. When the user requested options only, leave them as options. Carry material uncertainties into a specific next investigation instead of presenting a confident decision without support.

## Produce and check the brief

For a saved strategy or cross-team handoff, use [the positioning brief](references/positioning-brief.template.md), adapting the project's existing format. A small advisory request can finish with the relevant decision and proof gap in the response. Keep one authoritative brief; distinguish proposed and accepted choices, and reuse the current source rather than copying private records into it.

Trace every material audience, capability and performance claim to the supplied evidence. Walk a representative customer situation through the proposed audience, alternatives and promise; inspect whether the brief says something the business can actually deliver. If audience testing is requested, use neutral questions with the intended audience and record what was actually observed. An agent's interpretation is not a customer interview or proof of demand.

Before accepting a consequential strategy or durable shared brief, obtain a separate fresh-context assessment of the raw request, evidence, candidate and coverage. Retain the returned reviewer/session identity, evaluated revision, findings and unresolved limits; resolve defects and independently recheck affected claims. Without that assessment, return an unreviewed draft. Direct source and scope checks suffice for a small advisory answer that accepts no consequential decision, unless the user or project requires more. Human decisions and observed audience response remain separate from agent review.

Return the requested brief or options, recommendation where authorized, supporting evidence, accepted/proposed status and the exact unresolved choice or next test. Preparing positioning does not authorize publishing, changing accounts or rewriting unrelated content.

## Continue from the accepted result

Pass the accepted audience, promise, proof limits and current brand constraints to `design-brand-identity` for a requested visual system, `define-writing-voice` for language conventions or `plan-landing-page` for the page's argument. Check availability and describe the equivalent plain action if absent. Continue ready work already authorized; a positioning-only request ends with its brief and limitations.
