# Positioning brief

Use for a saved strategy or handoff. Adapt the authoritative project format and omit sections outside the request; this is not a mandatory questionnaire.

## Context and status

Business/offer, requested decision, owner, source revision/date, intended audience and current status. Identify existing commitments to preserve and which choices are open, delegated or accepted.

## Audience situation and alternatives

Describe the actual customer task or need, relevant buying/use context, current alternatives and source coverage. Separate observed behavior, quoted language and hypotheses; an invented persona is not audience evidence.

## Positions considered

| Position | Audience and situation | Meaningful difference | Proof available | Trade-off or gap |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A materially distinct option | Who needs what | What the business can deliver differently | Source and what it establishes | Why choose it; what could change the choice |

## Selected or proposed position

Record the audience, category or frame of reference, customer consequence, difference and promise in plain language. Include reasons to believe and delivery limits. A one-sentence statement can help a handoff; it need not become a public tagline.

## Claims and evidence

| Claim | Source/date | What the evidence establishes | Status and use limit |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Audience, capability or outcome claim | Located evidence | Observation, not an inferred result | Supported, hypothesis or unresolved; public/private limits |

## Checks and next decision

Record source checks, scenario walk-through, independent assessment where required and actual audience-testing coverage. Keep customer observations separate from agent judgments. Name the uncertainty, investigation, owner and observation that would change the recommendation; include acceptance status without implying unobserved approval.

## Handoff

Link the authoritative brief and safe proof sources. Carry the accepted promise, constraints and unresolved choices into the requested identity, voice or page-planning work; list unavailable prerequisites without inventing them.
