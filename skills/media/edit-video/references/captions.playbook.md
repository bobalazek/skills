# Captions on the final edit

Use this for caption-only work and for captions affected by an edit. Establish the language, destination and requested delivery: a sidecar track, embedded selectable track or captions burned into the image. Reuse the accepted treatment and corrected transcript. Check destination support before choosing a format; SRT and WebVTT do not guarantee identical styling across players.

## Correct the content

Listen to the supplied media or final mix and compare the transcript. Treat speech recognition as a draft. Verify names, numbers, negation and speaker changes; retain meaning and avoid replacing spoken words with marketing copy. Mark unresolved speech for review instead of inventing it. Accessibility captions include relevant non-speech sounds and speaker identification where the picture does not make them clear. Translation needs a check against the spoken meaning and the available reading time. [W3C's caption guidance](https://www.w3.org/WAI/media/av/captions/) explains these distinctions and why automatic captions need accuracy checks.

Keep caption correction separate from a request to alter speech. If the footage retains a word, deleting it from captions does not remove it from the audio. For a caption-only correction with settled timing, change only the necessary cues and preserve the existing video unless a new burned version is requested.

## Use final timing

Map corrected words through the final edit decisions or align them against the final audio. Recompute affected cues after cuts, rearrangements and speed changes; preserve the output occurrence when a source passage appears twice. Exclude removed speech, split cues crossing a cut and check that no cue refers to words outside its retained segment. A coarse source cue spanning deleted words needs realignment or retranscription of the affected output; endpoint arithmetic alone cannot recover its words' timing.

Check that cue starts and ends are ordered within the output duration, with positive durations and no unintended overlap. Break lines at readable phrase boundaries and preview dense passages at normal speed. Set text size, line length and holds from the actual composition and reading load; a platform preset is not proof of readability.

For burn-in, check contrast against changing backgrounds and avoid covering faces, essential screen content or existing labels. Recheck caption placement after reframing. Inspect current destination controls or an actual preview when safe areas matter; do not hard-code a universal margin. Keep a corrected editable caption source so a text repair does not require rebuilding from burned pixels.

## Verify the delivered track

Open the exported track with its exact companion video in a compatible player, or play the actual burned export. Check text and speech together at the start and end, each edit boundary, speaker changes and dense or uncertain passages. Listen through the captioned output to establish full content accuracy; if only samples were checked, name that coverage and leave the rest unverified. A parser or timestamp check cannot establish transcription accuracy, readable holds or lip sync.

Record language, media identity, caption revision, player and observed coverage. If audio access or language competence is missing, deliver only the requested draft with those gaps and identify the required listening or language review. Keep caption-only work at its requested scope; no new edit, publishing workflow or extra review document is needed.
