# Carousel craft

Use while writing or reworking a carousel's sequence, cover or panel system. It covers what makes a reader stop, swipe and finish. Formats, counts and crops come from the destination's current documentation, and the project's brand and voice guide take precedence over these defaults.

## Earn the swipe on the cover

The cover competes in a feed at thumbnail size, usually beside video and other covers. Give it one promise that the rest of the set keeps: a specific outcome, a question the audience already has, or a supported fact that changes how they see the topic. An outcome ("Cut your onboarding to one screen") usually gives a clearer reason to swipe than a container ("5 onboarding tips").

Use few, large words. As a rule of thumb, the headline should be readable in a glance, often under about eight words, with at most one supporting line. Show that more follows with a cue the destination's interface does not cover, such as a panel count, an arrow, or an element crossing the right edge. View the exported cover at the width it actually appears in a phone feed and in any grid or profile crop the destination applies; simplify it until the headline reads there. Never promise more than the set delivers.

## One idea per panel

Give each panel one idea a reader could screenshot and pass on: a step, a point, a comparison or a figure. A panel holds a headline and a sentence or two of support. Explanation that needs more belongs in the companion caption or a linked resource. Remove filler panels that only announce what is coming or repeat the cover.

## Progression and payoff

Pick a shape that fits the material and make the order visible:

| Pattern | Panel sequence | Payoff panel |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Steps | Numbered actions in the order a reader performs them | The finished result |
| Points | Numbered tips, mistakes or lessons | The most useful point, or a summary to keep |
| Before and after | The problem state, then the change | The after state, shown rather than claimed |
| Myth and fact | A common belief, then the correction with its source | The rule to remember |
| Story | Situation, turn, outcome | The lesson or result |
| Data walk-through | One chart point per panel | The conclusion and its source |

Number panels when order matters. Each panel can leave a small open question that the next one answers; do not use a cliffhanger the content cannot pay off. Put the payoff panel, which delivers the cover's promise explicitly, just before the action.

## Readable at feed size

Design at the export canvas, but judge at display size. On a phone, a panel commonly appears only a few hundred CSS pixels wide, so set type sizes by inspecting exports at that width, not by a fixed point size. Keep lines short and contrast high. Put a scrim or solid field behind text over photographs. Keep text out of areas the destination overlays with counters, buttons or captions, checked against its current documentation or an actual preview. Simplify charts to the few values that make the point, label them directly and enlarge the labels.

## One system across panels

Keep the grid, margins, type scale, color roles and headline position the same on every panel, with recurring elements such as a panel number or brand mark in a fixed place. Use one accent color for emphasis. Vary the content within that system, alternating text-led and visual-led panels for rhythm, without moving the anchors. An element that crosses a panel edge can pull the reader onward; check that it splits cleanly at the boundary and that both panels still read alone. The cover and final panel can depart from the grid deliberately, but they keep the brand and type system.

## State the action on the final panel

End with one action the destination supports and the owner wants: follow, save, visit a specific page, sign up, or reply with a keyword when someone will act on replies. Name the specific destination, such as the exact page address. Text in an image is not a link, so put the address where the destination allows links and keep the companion caption consistent with the panel. Restate the payoff in one line beside the action so a reader who lands on the last panel still gets the point.
