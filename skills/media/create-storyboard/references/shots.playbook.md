# Plan shots and visual continuity

## Give each shot a job

Start with what the viewer must understand, notice or feel. Map the accepted script's opening, useful continuation, body and payoff to visible events. Name the opening state, action/change and ending state, even when the intended action is a readable hold or quiet observation. Choose shot count from coverage and rhythm, not a fixed panel quota.

For example, a script promising to show a repaired hinge needs the relevant fault, the meaningful repair step and evidence that it now moves correctly. Three angles of a closed cabinet do not supply that sequence. If only those stills exist, record the missing action or agree a narrower promise.

For a feed opening, plan the first visual, spoken line and visible text as one understandable promise. Map the immediate continuation to the first useful action, proof or consequence, then locate the actual payoff. Each needs a source or production gap. Let title and picture complement each other; do not spend several opening panels repeating the same teaser. For silent work, the visual sequence must carry this progression itself.

## Choose framing and movement

Use framing to reveal the subject, relationship or detail the beat needs. Preserve enough context to orient the viewer; a close-up needs a clear referent. Motivated movement can reveal a detail or follow action; decorative movement cannot substitute for evidence. Compose each requested aspect ratio around the essential action, text and platform controls instead of assuming an automatic crop.

For product footage, preserve genuine UI states and exact values. Capture missing states rather than retouching evidence. For diagrams and motion graphics, record the relationship or change the animation explains. For silent work, picture and readable text must carry what narration would otherwise explain.

## Preserve continuity

Track recurring subject identity, location, props, screen direction, position, action state and light when they matter to comprehension. An exit state must connect sensibly to the next opening or make the change deliberate. Reusable character/set references need selected files and versions, not only descriptive names. Do not create a character bible or asset library for a one-off shot without a continuity need.

## Place sound and timing

Record dialogue/narration, native ambience or effects, music and intended silence separately. For visible dialogue, identify the speaking subject and how performance and picture will stay synchronized. For external narration, use selected audio to set final cues; until then label estimated duration and allow time for visible actions, pauses and text. Hold a teaching step long enough to inspect its essential action; place reveals where the relevant information becomes visible. Decorative cuts or motion must not hide that information or create unsupported performance claims. Music rights and availability remain production prerequisites, not assumptions.

## Resolve the production path

Map each shot to inspected existing media or a missing production action. State exact source/reference files and their purpose, what remains unknown, and the owner/tool required to resolve it. A generation prompt is not proof that the output performs the action; production must inspect the actual result. Missing reference attachments, unresolved likeness rights, unavailable locations or absent demonstrations remain explicit gaps.

## Preview the sequence

At the requested fidelity, inspect all panels, important action states and joins. Check the opening promise against the last meaningful beat, readable text at destination size and continuity across the sequence. A contact sheet can check still coverage and composition; it cannot establish motion, sound or pacing. Pass the accepted board version, asset identities, cue dependencies and unresolved choices to production; changes invalidate affected downstream work.
