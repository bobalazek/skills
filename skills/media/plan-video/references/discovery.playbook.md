# Discovery and human collaboration

Use for an open brief or a change that alters the intended video. Reuse answered questions and project conventions. Ask the next small group of consequential questions, then adapt to the answers; do not dump the whole guide as a questionnaire or decide a personal creative preference silently.

For each open choice, make clear what answer is needed and which next step waits for it. Do not ask a question and start the dependent work in the same turn using your own answer. Preserve the user's reply in the existing brief/conversation, reflect any changed direction, and resume only the work it resolves. Requested exploratory options can remain proposals; an unanswered question, a labeled assumption or elapsed time does not approve a choice.

## Purpose: what must this accomplish?

Clarify why this video should exist now and what success means to its owner. Does the viewer need to learn, decide, act, feel something or simply enjoy it? Which message or experience must survive every revision, and what would make the piece fail? Distinguish an editorial objective from a measurable outcome; if performance matters, identify the available signal and its limits rather than guaranteeing views or conversions.

## Audience: who is on the other side?

Ask who the intended viewer is, what they already know, what they care about and what context they arrive with. Identify language, cultural context, accessibility and likely sound-on/muted viewing where they matter. Ask whose needs take priority when audiences conflict; do not assume experts and first-time viewers need the same explanation.

## Type and experience: what are we making?

Establish the subject and whether the video is factual, fictional, instructional, documentary, promotional, conversational or another kind. Separate purpose from narrative format and visual treatment. Ask about tone, emotional arc, presenter/character preferences, useful examples and what the owner dislikes. When they are undecided, offer a few concrete directions with tradeoffs. Reflect what was learned before drafting a full story.

## Destination: where and how will it be watched?

Keep purpose, format and channel as separate decisions. Personal memories, a creator story, a product demonstration or an educational lesson describe different purposes/content; YouTube, TikTok and Instagram describe destinations. A destination does not automatically make the work promotional, narrated or fast-cut.

Clarify the actual surface: for example, a YouTube watch-page video versus Shorts, an Instagram Reel versus Story or feed placement, a TikTok post, a product-page embed, or a privately shared video. Ask whether this is one primary piece or several requested adaptations. Carry the audience's arrival context, expected length, framing, sound/caption needs, title/cover/first-frame promise and intended action into the brief. A short cut may need a new opening and a self-contained payoff, not just cropping or truncating the master. Private/personal work may prioritize consent, intimacy and a limited audience over reach.

Use the user's accepted channel and purpose when supplied. If they want help choosing, compare fit, available assets and production/review capacity before recommending; do not manufacture algorithm or conversion guarantees. Verify current platform delivery limits and requirements when they determine a production decision, rather than freezing duration, safe-area, codec or disclosure rules into the skill.

## Material and constraints: what can support it?

Identify available facts, footage, product access, people, locations, illustrations, music and brand references. Ask which material is fixed, what can be created and what must never appear. Establish destination, length range, aspect ratios, language variants, deadline, production/review capacity and budget when these affect feasibility. Keep privacy, consent, rights and provider spending boundaries explicit without turning a low-cost idea session into an operations checklist.

## Brainstorm: explore, then choose together

Offer distinct premises with a viewer reward, plausible hook, intended payoff and main production tradeoff. Discuss why each fits the answers rather than assigning invented performance scores. Invite the human to select, combine, reject or sharpen them. If no option fits, use the rejection reason to revise the premise or audience assumption; more cosmetic variants will not fix the mismatch.

Before dependent work, reflect the selected concept, format, tone and story promise/payoff in a short summary and obtain confirmation when it has not already been given or delegated. An explicit request to choose and proceed delegates that choice; record it. If a choice remains open, label drafts exploratory and continue only work independent of it. Do not claim approval from elapsed time, a key in the environment or an agent review.

## Preserve decisions through the pipeline

Keep a compact log in the existing brief/project record: original request and clarifications; question and answer; options considered and why selected/parked; accepted direction and who accepted it; open choices and the next checkpoint. Link source artifacts and versions. Do not preserve unnecessary private conversation or create another tracker when the chat/brief already carries the record. Before a handoff, summarize durable decisions there so a later session can resume without guessing.

Use human checkpoints at meaningful boundaries: chosen concept and promise before developing the full story; script/message/payoff before dependent recording or boards that assume fixed words; visual direction and material shot choices before costly production. These can be combined for a small job, reused when already accepted, or delegated explicitly. Factual checks and independent quality review do not substitute for the human's creative choice. A user choosing the story also does not prove that an export plays correctly.

When feedback changes a settled decision, log what changed and why, preserve still-valid choices, and identify affected script beats, shots, recordings and exports. Ask again only about the changed decision, not the whole brief. Finish with the next unresolved choice or requested artifact, not an automatic production batch.
