# Timed storyboard

Reuse accepted script and measured audio. Mark scratch/estimated cues; update dependent visuals and captions after a recording changes. Cover every material beat and join rather than enforcing a panel count.

| Beat | Frame/time range | Supported script/message | Visual and screen text | Audio/cue source | Asset/reference | Entry → exit / transition | Preview and unresolved choice |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| B1 | Start inclusive, end exclusive | Claim and source | Composition, text, hold | Actual recording cue or labeled estimate | Approved asset/version | Shared anchor and next join | Frame path, status, owner |

## Preview checks

- Show representative style frames, each beat and boundary states at the intended aspect and destination size.
- Check reading time, safe placement, contrast and captions with actual text; preview the longest label and densest scene.
- Keep color/shape meanings and hero details consistent. Use motion to explain a relationship or change; remove movement that competes with the message.
- Compare references beside original frames when helpful; record unmatched traits and required attribution.
- Reconcile human choices that block animation. Preserve accepted decisions and label unresolved drafts.

## Animation handoff

Pass the brief, script/audio versions, frame ranges, layout/style rules, assets and exit states. Independent scenes need separate ownership/output paths. Final assembly waits for required inputs and checks every join. The storyboard is a preview artifact, not export or audio-sync proof.
