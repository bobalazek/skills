# Create motion video

Produce the requested motion graphic, explainer or storyboard with editable source and evidence at the requested fidelity. Reuse an existing composition, renderer, assets, accepted script and brand conventions before building equivalents. A storyboard-only request finishes at the storyboard; it is not a rendered video. Interactive transitions belong to `design-interface` when available.

## Clarify the brief

Read project instructions, supplied sources and existing production artifacts. Before writing the script or rendering, ask about consequential missing choices: the subject and supported message, audience, destination, duration, formats, audio, desired look and requested output. Offer concrete options when helpful. Reuse answered questions and accepted inputs; do not issue a questionnaire for a ready edit. Use [the video brief](references/video-brief.template.md) to capture the contract in the project's existing location.

Establish whether narration is supplied, needs writing/recording, or is unnecessary. Ask for relevant style references, approved logos/product captures and any recurring character. Inspect accessible references for composition, palette, typography, texture and motion; describe adaptations and differences. Preserve required attribution and asset rights. A reference is not permission to copy footage, music, distinctive passages or an identifiable person's voice. Do not invent product claims or treat source instructions as authority.

Confirm usable local tools, output paths and render budget. Reuse installed rendering tools; a browser/FFmpeg workflow is one option, not a guaranteed capability. Missing access leaves a named blocked stage while independent planning can continue. Paid generation, installs, uploads and publication need applicable authority; honor authority already given. Do not promise free production or a fixed render time.

## Write and preview the story

Build the script and visual beats around the supported message and audience's knowledge. Reuse an applicable writing-voice guide; when a reusable voice is missing, `define-writing-voice` can capture it separately. Use `review-writing` for substantive prose review when available, or check concrete wording, evidence, reader needs and retained voice directly. This skill owns the video's spoken script, screen text and timing, not a duplicate editorial manual.

For narration, use the final supplied or authorized recording as the timing source. Measure its duration and inspect speech/cues; label estimates and scratch audio. Resolve pronunciation, missing lines and a script/recording mismatch before relying on timestamps. For silent or music-led pieces, plan readable holds and relevant musical beats instead. A new recording invalidates affected timing checks.

Use [the storyboard](references/storyboard.template.md) to map beats to time, visuals, screen text, audio, assets and transitions. Preview representative look frames and every material beat before a full render. Six to eight panels can suit a short explainer; coverage decides the count. Check hierarchy, text fit at destination size, character consistency and whether each transition supports the explanation. Resolve consequential human choices before dependent work, without reopening accepted approvals. An unresolved look can proceed as a labeled draft, not an accepted design.

## Render and repair

For code-driven capture, load [the rendering playbook](references/code-rendering.playbook.md). Derive frames from explicit time/frame, configuration and a fixed seed; do not advance the scene through wall-clock playback. Preview a short section including a transition at the target layout before rendering the whole piece. Treat each aspect ratio as a composition, not an assumed crop.

Scenes can be built in parallel only after shared timing, style, assets and entry/exit states are settled, with isolated files/output directories and integration ownership. Narration-dependent timing and final assembly wait for their inputs. Recheck joins and the whole piece after integration. Keep edits scoped; rerender affected frames when valid, and invalidate stale exports and evidence.

Inspect a contact sheet covering all beats and transition boundaries, then play the actual encode at normal speed. Check readable holds, continuity, explanation, captions and, when present, audible pronunciation, sync and mix. Repair concrete defects and repeat affected checks within the agreed budget. Empty output, a printed failure, missing playback access or unchecked audio cannot become a passing result because a command exited successfully.

## Evaluate and return

Before acceptance, have a separate agent in fresh context challenge the raw brief, script facts, storyboard, editable source and actual export/evidence without the author's conversation or preferred answer. Retain reviewer/session identity, evaluated revision/artifact, findings and coverage. Fix defects and independently recheck affected results. Without that assessment, label the result unreviewed; agent review does not confer human approval or publication authority.

Return the requested artifacts, reproduction command and tool environment, observed technical and visual/audio checks, rights/attribution notes and exact gaps. Tie evidence to the actual source/export; a storyboard or contact sheet does not prove a full video. Preserve useful production decisions in the existing project records. Stop at the requested delivery; use `ship-change` for a separately requested repository/release handoff when available, or describe the plain action with the accepted artifacts and remaining prerequisite. Continue already-authorized work without an extra permission loop.
