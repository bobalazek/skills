# Coding ledger

Use for mixed sources, uncertain identities or reported counts. Keep it in the approved research record; for a small corpus, combine these fields in one table.

## Corpus and counting rules

- Question, intended audience, included period and product versions.
- Sources and export/filter rules; included, excluded and unavailable records with reasons.
- Unit reported for each count: source records, distinct events, identified people or accounts.
- Identity evidence, unresolved matches and exclusions from unique-person/account counts.
- Eligible denominator for each theme or segment; which records were actually reviewed or asked about the topic.
- Code definitions and inclusion/exclusion boundaries; multi-label policy and material coding changes.
- Source-access restrictions and redaction applied to the shared result.

## Evidence ledger

| Evidence ID and source locator | Date, channel and context | Person/account ID or unknown | Observation and evidence type | Requested solution | Codes | Duplicate or identity decision |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Source record plus exact location | Question/task, segment and version where known | Pseudonymous IDs backed by permitted identifiers | Exact quote, observed action, reported experience or attributed summary | Preserve separately, or none | Defined codes; multiple allowed | Canonical record, related event, merge basis or unresolved |

Keep identities in restricted storage when needed. A quoted name or matching display name alone does not establish that records belong to one person. Do not infer sensitive demographics to fill a segment field.

## Needs record

| Need ID, user and context | Desired outcome and barrier | Supporting evidence IDs | Opposing evidence or alternative explanation | Corpus counts and limits | Unanswered question |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Stable ID; observed segment | State what the person needs to accomplish; mark interpretation | Include locators through the ledger | Preserve different conditions and dissent | Unit, numerator/denominator, period, unresolved identity and sampling limits | Smallest missing evidence that could change the finding |

## Counting examples

These are synthetic examples, not evidence about a product.

- One interview excerpt copied into two slide decks is one observation with three source pointers.
- Three tickets about the same failed export by one verified person are three records, one event and one person. Separate failures would establish recurrence; neither case adds people.
- Two verified people linked to the same account count as two people and one account. An anonymous review with similar wording remains an unresolved identity; it cannot add a verified unique person or be silently merged.
- With ten identified participants, seven mentioning need A and six mentioning need B, including four who mention both, report A as 7/10, B as 6/10 and either as 9/10. The label percentages total 130% because they overlap. These counts describe that corpus only; silence in the other records does not establish absence of a need.

## Method sources

These sources support the observation/interpretation split, outcome-based needs and privacy handling. The ledger's counting examples make those checks concrete for mixed feedback; the sources do not establish a universal scoring or sampling rule.

- [GOV.UK: Analyse a research session](https://www.gov.uk/service-manual/user-research/analyse-a-research-session) separates source observations from findings and proposed actions.
- [GOV.UK: Learning about users and their needs](https://www.gov.uk/service-manual/user-research/start-by-learning-user-needs) describes evidence-based needs around the user's desired outcome.
- [GOV.UK: Managing user research data and participant privacy](https://www.gov.uk/service-manual/user-research/managing-user-research-data-participant-privacy) covers permitted use, limited access and privacy when sharing research.
