# Validation record

Use the relevant fields in the existing brief or decision record. A small decision may need only a few paragraphs; omit fields that do not affect it.

## Decision and evidence

- **Decision:** commitment being considered, owner, cost, reversibility and constraints.
- **Audience and problem:** users, buyers, situation, intended outcome and current alternatives.
- **Claim:** the assumption under examination and credible competing explanation.
- **Evidence:** source locator and date, segment, observation, interpretation, contrary evidence and coverage limits. Identify inherited summaries that cannot be checked against raw records.
- **Assessment:** what is supported, what remains unknown and why the next decision depends on it.

## Match the method

| Uncertainty | Useful evidence | What it does not establish |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Does this problem occur and matter? | Existing behavior, support records, neutral accounts of recent real incidents and workarounds | Representative frequency without a suitable sample and denominator |
| Why would people change their current approach? | Observed switching barriers, actual alternatives and relevant user accounts | Adoption simply because competitors lack a feature |
| Can people complete the proposed task? | Observed tasks with an appropriate prototype or working flow | Willingness to buy or continued use |
| Will a buyer commit? | An accurately described authorized pilot, purchase or other meaningful commitment | Retention or sustainable economics from initial interest alone |
| Can we deliver it within constraints? | A bounded technical or operational experiment and checked cost assumptions | Demand from technical feasibility |

Interviews should explore a recent experience, what happened and why, before asking about a proposed solution. Preserve the actual question when it may have prompted agreement. A hypothetical answer remains hypothetical.

## Next test, if needed

- **Hypothesis and alternative:** what different observations would distinguish them?
- **Method and audience:** source or participants, selection rationale and expected limitations.
- **Measure:** observed behavior, unit, eligible denominator, collection conditions and time window.
- **Decision criteria:** success, failure and inconclusive outcomes, their rationale, and the action each would support. Cover mixed outcomes such as purchase without use; distinguish unavailable observation or failed delivery from evidence against demand. Mark thresholds chosen by the decision owner as choices rather than empirical facts.
- **Execution:** owner, bounded effort/cost, needed access and authority, data handling and stopping conditions.
- **Observed result:** only after execution; evidence locations, actual conditions, deviations and unresolved gaps.
- **Decision:** proceed with which commitment, revise, stop or investigate; strongest counterevidence and what could change the decision.
- **Review:** actual independent reviewer/session, candidate revision, returned findings, resolved defects and remaining limits. Human approval, when required, is recorded separately.

## Method references

[GOV.UK discovery research](https://www.gov.uk/service-manual/user-research/user-research-in-discovery) describes learning about users' current experience and combining existing data with research. Its [interview guidance](https://www.gov.uk/service-manual/user-research/using-in-depth-interviews) supports preparing a discussion guide and learning from participants' experiences. Apply the relevant methods to the project; these sources do not validate the particular idea or impose a universal sample size.
