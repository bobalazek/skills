# Technology selection

Fix decision criteria before comparing tools. Use the project's actual constraints: deployment, data, latency, team familiarity, maintainability, accessibility, security, integration fit, operating burden, migration effort, and exit cost.

| Option | Requirement fit | Evidence and date | Costs/limits | Reversibility | Unresolved questions |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Current or simplest suitable approach | Specific capability | Inspected configuration or primary documentation | Build/run/maintenance implications | Switching and data portability | What must be proved |

Evaluate only credible alternatives. A numeric score needs an explicit scale and meaningful weights; prose trade-offs are often clearer. Distinguish preferred conventions from hard requirements.

For a starter or software factory, inspect shipped capabilities, extension points, update strategy, local customizations, licensing, and operating responsibilities. Configure and verify suitable existing capabilities instead of rebuilding them.

Verify the chosen versions together. Do not infer compatibility from separate marketing pages or assume that newest means suitable. Record the accepted choice, rejected alternatives, evidence, and the condition that would justify revisiting it.
