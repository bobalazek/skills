# Usability study playbook

Use the relevant parts when a task protocol or synthesis needs a durable record. Reuse the project's research format; do not create empty sections or collect participant details without a purpose.

## Protocol

Record the decision and what evidence could change it, target revision and fidelity, relevant audience/context, task scope, and intended observation method. Name known limitations such as an unavailable backend, incomplete prototype branch, unsupported assistive technology or supplied recording that starts mid-task.

For each task, define:

| Field | What to record |
| --- | --- |
| Goal and starting state | What the person needs to accomplish and what they initially know or have |
| Neutral prompt | The situation and desired outcome, without the interface label or expected route |
| Success evidence | The visible or terminal result that demonstrates the goal, including comprehension when relevant |
| Observation | Actions, hesitation, errors, assistance, abandonment and recovery that matter to the decision |
| Stop condition | Participant choice, agreed task boundary, unsafe action or environment failure |
| Follow-up | A neutral question about an observed action or expectation |

For example, a task about finding a past export can ask the person to retrieve a previously created file for a colleague. Telling them to open the Exports tab would disclose the navigation answer. If the question concerns whether a label is understood, test its meaning without teaching the label first.

For a tree test, retain the tested hierarchy and label version without the full visual interface. For a comprehension task, capture the participant's explanation before providing clarification. Do not score a person's preference as successful completion of a different behavioral task.

## Session conduct

Confirm the permitted activity and data handling before starting. Use a participant code in working notes; keep any necessary identity mapping in its approved location. Recording is optional unless required by the agreed study; contemporaneous notes can be evidence when their limits are explicit. Do not upload records to a transcription or analysis provider without the required permission.

Rehearse the task and reset procedure. Keep fixtures independent when one participant's actions could change another's starting state. Concurrent sessions need separate accounts or environments where state conflicts. Reset only study-owned state.

Observe before asking for explanation. Allow the participant to stop or decline. Record moderator hints and task changes; assisted completion is a different outcome from unaided completion. A broken test environment is a study limitation, not automatically a participant failure or evidence that the design is unusable.

## Evidence record

Use one row per relevant observation, with timestamps or artifact locations when available:

| Session and task | Starting conditions | Observed action/result | Assistance or deviation | Evidence pointer |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Anonymized code; task ID | Revision, device/input context, fixture and known limitations | What happened, with exact quotes only when recorded | Hint, interruption, task change or missing segment | Approved note, clip or recording offset |

Do not turn a paraphrase into a quotation. Redact identifiers from shared extracts and preserve access/retention restrictions. If required evidence cannot be shared with a reviewer, record that review limitation rather than distributing it anyway.

## Findings and decisions

Group observations by the barrier or unmet expectation, not by participant. For each finding, give affected task/audience, evidence, consequence, confidence, contrary observations and the proposed next action. Keep the proposed design separate from the observed problem; one failure can have several possible remedies.

When reporting a fraction, state whether its denominator is participants, attempted tasks or recorded sessions. Exclude neither failures nor missing observations silently. Report missing data separately. With repeat sessions, distinguish a new participant from another attempt by the same person; prior exposure may affect comparison.

For before/after claims, retain comparable task difficulty, starting state, audience/context and outcome definition. Document unavoidable differences and learning effects. Do not claim statistical significance without an appropriate design and analysis. A directional finding can still justify a scoped change without inventing a population estimate.

## Challenge before acceptance

- Could someone follow the task wording to the answer without understanding the interface?
- Does claimed success require a persisted or terminal result that was never observed?
- Did a hint, prototype shortcut, recording gap or changed fixture account for the result?
- Are severe minority barriers or contradictory observations hidden by an aggregate?
- Does a recommendation follow from evidence, or is it the author's prior preference?
- Are participants, devices, access needs and untested branches described accurately?

A plan is finished when its tasks, environment, observation method, permissions and unresolved prerequisites are clear enough for the intended study. Findings are finished when material claims are traceable, contradictions and limits are visible, and the requested decision can be informed without invented evidence.
