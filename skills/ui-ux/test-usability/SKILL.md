# Test usability

Produce the requested study protocol or findings about how people perform a defined task. Start from available evidence: a planning request needs a usable protocol; supplied sessions may need analysis without new recruitment. A plan, agent walkthrough, or simulated persona is not evidence that users succeeded.

## Frame the study

Identify the decision the study should inform, relevant user roles and contexts, target interface or prototype revision, and consequential tasks. Read accepted requirements, previous findings, and actual product behavior where available. Preserve relevant existing workflows and distinguish a prototype limitation from a product failure.

Choose a method that answers the decision: task observation for completion and recovery, a tree test for navigation findability, or a comprehension task for labels and consequences. Interviews can explain expectations but do not prove task completion. Select participants and study depth for the question and relevant differences; do not impose a universal sample count or promise statistical confidence from a small qualitative study.

When preparing tasks or synthesizing multiple sessions, use [the study playbook](references/study.playbook.md) for a protocol, observation record, and checks against misleading conclusions. Keep the study focused on the requested decision rather than expanding into general market research.

## Prepare and observe

Check participant access, tool availability, a safe test environment, and permission for the intended study activities. Recruitment messages, recording, sharing, or live-account actions require their actual authorization. Follow the agreed consent, storage, access and retention arrangements; explain the activity before participation or recording and honor withdrawal. Use synthetic or approved data and avoid real charges, messages, credential disclosure or irreversible actions.

Give realistic goals without naming the control or path being tested. Define observable success and failure before the session. Rehearse the environment and instructions, labeling rehearsal separately from participant evidence. During authorized sessions, record what the participant attempted, what happened, assistance, recovery and uncertainty. Do not coach a successful outcome or quietly change tasks between participants; retain material deviations with the evidence.

If people, consent, access or recordings are unavailable, continue useful protocol work or analyze the evidence actually supplied. State precisely what cannot be established. A request to run a study remains incomplete without the required observations; a requested plan can be complete as a plan.

## Synthesize and verify

Link findings to specific tasks, anonymized session references, observed actions and capture conditions. Keep participant statements, observed behavior, interpretation and proposed changes distinct. Preserve conflicting evidence and isolated severe barriers rather than voting them away. Report assisted completion, unsuccessful attempts and missing observations separately.

Use counts and denominators only when supported by the records. Explain coverage limits; do not generalize convenience participants to an entire audience or attribute improvement to a design change without a comparable baseline. A usability study does not establish comprehensive accessibility conformance. Limit recommendations to the evidence and identify the next observation that would resolve a consequential uncertainty.

Before acceptance, have a separate agent in fresh context challenge the protocol or findings against the raw request, task definitions, candidate artifact and permitted source evidence, without the author's conversation or preferred conclusion. Respect participant consent and access limits when sharing evidence with a reviewer. It must check task neutrality, evidence traceability, unsupported completion claims and conflicting observations. Reconcile demonstrated defects and independently recheck affected results after fixes. If independent review or required evidence is unavailable, label the result unreviewed or incomplete rather than ready. Human approval remains separate.

## Communicate the result

Match the requested audience, tone and depth, then project conventions. Return the protocol or findings, the decision it supports, method and coverage, observed proof, exact gaps and next action. Update the existing authorized research record rather than creating duplicate summaries. For authorized PR work, include relevant redacted observations and independent findings when opening the PR, using reviewer-accessible evidence and refreshing it after material changes. Do not publish participant records merely to make a PR self-contained.

Next: use `map-user-flows` when the evidence calls for changed navigation or recovery, `design-interface` for an agreed screen revision, or `write-spec` when required behavior needs agreement. `review-interface` addresses a separate heuristic or rendered assessment. Pass the supported finding, affected tasks and revision, evidence limits and unresolved choice; check skill availability and describe the plain action when absent. Stop at the requested research result, or continue ready work already authorized.
