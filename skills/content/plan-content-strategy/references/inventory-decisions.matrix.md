# Content inventory decisions

Use the existing inventory format. Record only fields needed for the decision: item/location, reader need, current purpose, evidence and date, treatment, priority/reason, owner and missing prerequisite. Unknown analytics or ownership stay unknown.

| Treatment | Evidence to inspect | Decision and consequence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Keep | Useful accurate material, served reader need, current terms and relevant usage where available | Explain what already works; avoid rewriting it merely for consistency |
| Update | A useful topic with specific stale facts, missing answers or unclear wording | Name the gap, preserved facts and evidence required for the update |
| Combine | Substantial reader-purpose overlap, source differences, links and distinct audience needs | Propose the destination and retained unique material; do not erase a useful separate task |
| Retire | Superseded or invalid purpose, maintenance burden, affected readers and dependencies | Identify replacement/archive and link or redirect investigation; traffic alone cannot decide removal, and proposing retirement grants no write authority |
| Create | An unmet supported reader need, absent coverage and feasible evidence/production inputs | Explain why existing material cannot satisfy it and what would make the assignment ready |

Search coverage is one possible input. Keep observed query data, untested query ideas and unavailable demand evidence distinct. A missing article is not automatically an opportunity; a legal or regulated-content question needs qualified project evidence rather than a universal list of required pages.

A priority rationale can state: “Update the setup guide first: the supplied tickets repeatedly ask the unanswered prerequisite question; a maintainer can verify it now. Defer the comparison article until the alternative's current behavior is sourced.” This is a synthetic decision pattern, not a universal priority order.

For recurring content, identify who can maintain time-sensitive facts and what event or agreed interval triggers review. Choose useful observable signals tied to the goal, with known measurement limits; defining instrumentation or claiming causal improvement is separate work.
