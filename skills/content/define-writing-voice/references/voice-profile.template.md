# Writing voice profile

Use in the consuming project's existing voice guide, or `docs/voice.md` when no owner exists. Fill relevant fields from evidence; do not publish an empty template or duplicate an existing brand guide. The source skill's references folder is not where project-specific profiles belong.

## Identity, scope and status

Record the person or brand, intended readers, channels, language/locale, profile owner if known, last reviewed date and status. Distinguish observed style, proposed direction and explicitly accepted conventions. State consequential unresolved choices rather than implying approval. Link the guide from the existing docs index; keep agent instructions to a conditional pointer.

## Source coverage

| Source URL or project path | Authorship and date, when known | What was read and why it represents the voice | Limits or exclusions |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Supplied writing sample | Confirmed person/brand, guest or unknown | Passage/page and relevant audience/channel | Access gap, older style, translation or unrepresentative material |

For a blog or account, record inspected pages/posts and sample scope, not a claim to have read the whole corpus. Deduplicate reposts. Use short, necessary excerpts with their locations; keep private sources in the authorized record. Link to source content instead of copying whole articles.

## Voice traits and language conventions

| Trait or convention | Evidence or explicit preference | What a writer should do | Boundary and status |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| E.g. dry humor, direct explanation or professional register | Exact short passage or attributed preference | Observable word, sentence or structure choice | When it fits, when to adapt, and observed/proposed/accepted status |

Include relevant choices for perspective (`I`, `we`, audience address), vocabulary and terminology, contractions, sentence rhythm, technical depth, anecdotes, headings, punctuation, profanity and humor. Record only choices supported or requested; no fixed trait count or default witty personality. Identify phrasing the owner wants preserved or avoided, with a reason. Keep preferred expressions distinct from facts or opinions that new work still needs to establish.

## Tone by situation

| Audience/channel/situation | Useful adjustment | Meaning or behavior to protect |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Blog, social post, product page, support, error or other relevant situation | How direct, warm, playful or formal to be, with an example when useful | Factual limits, reader needs, supported recovery, approved legal text |

Do not assume a language or channel inherits every convention from another. Mark missing examples. A brand can be playful while a consequential error still needs a clear outcome and recovery path.

## Examples and checks

Show a representative source excerpt and what it demonstrates, followed by a short application using supplied facts or explicitly fictional content. Label source quotations and new illustrations separately. Give the concrete reason an off-voice alternative fails; an example is guidance, not a phrase to repeat everywhere.

Retain source checks, application results, independent assessment identity/revision/findings/coverage, open human choices and a refresh trigger. A review can validate fidelity to supplied evidence; approval by the person or brand owner must be recorded separately. Update this guide in place when accepted conventions change.

## Reference

[Mailchimp's voice and tone guide](https://styleguide.mailchimp.com/voice-and-tone/) illustrates a stable voice with tone adapted to context. Checked on 2026-10-07. Use that distinction, rather than importing Mailchimp's personality into every project.
