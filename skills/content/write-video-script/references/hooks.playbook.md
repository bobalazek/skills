# Hooks and immediate continuation

Use for the opening of a video or chapter, and for a bounded hook rewrite. These are craft options, not guarantees of reach or retention. Reuse an accepted format and honest packaging promise.

## 1. Confirm the promise

Write down what the viewer expects from the title/cover or opening frame, and where the actual piece fulfills it. An opening cannot repair a concept with no useful answer. If the material supports only a narrow result, narrow the promise. A hypothetical example, staged reconstruction or proposed experiment must not masquerade as observed proof.

## 2. Make the hook understandable

Give the viewer an intelligible subject, a reason it matters to them and a credible reason to continue. Remove delayed context, ambiguous references, irrelevant stakes and curiosity with no substance. Prefer concrete action or language to a credential introduction. Curiosity may come from an unanswered question, an action in progress, a useful demonstration or a supported contrast.

| Opening choice | Use when | What must be real |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Demonstrate the result | A visible outcome creates interest | The result and its material conditions |
| Start a test or investigation | A question has an observable answer | The method, evidence and limits |
| Teach a useful step | The viewer recognizes the task | An actionable step, not only an unexplained label |
| Open on action or tension | A story changes through consequences | Relevant stakes and a later resolution |
| Contrast an expectation | Evidence supports a surprising difference | Both the expectation and the narrower supported claim |
| Establish an experience | Atmosphere or reflection is the purpose | A perceptible direction, without forcing a sales promise |

## 3. Align words and picture

Write the first visual, spoken opening and optional on-screen text together. They should reinforce one promise, not compete with unrelated claims. They need not repeat identical words. When footage is fixed, build around what it actually shows. When muted viewing matters, inspect whether picture/text make the subject legible without sound; do not claim that inspection from a text-only draft. Keep overlays readable in the target layout and clear of the action and player controls.

## 4. Write the immediate continuation

After attracting attention, show why staying is worthwhile. Deliver a first useful step, real proof, a consequence or necessary context. A tutorial can go straight into the first step; a story can follow the opening action's consequence. Avoid restarting the hook, stretching a credential preamble or chaining empty teasers. Address a real objection if it blocks understanding, without inventing an objection merely to use a formula.

## 5. Compare and repair

Evaluate alternatives against clarity, relevance, support and fit with the actual continuation and ending. Do not rank them as proven performers without audience evidence. For a scoped request, give the requested options with a short explanation and the necessary visual/continuation context; do not write a full campaign. If all options need unavailable proof, repair the premise before intensifying the wording.
