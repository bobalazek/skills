# Story, payoff and performance

Use only the stages needed for the requested outline, script or revision. An existing accepted hook or body can remain unchanged while its ending is repaired.

## Outline: know the destination

Decide the answer, changed situation or intended experience before filling pages. Connect the opening promise to that ending through necessary evidence and events. For teaching, preserve prerequisites. For a narrative, ask what each action causes or changes; a sequence of unrelated events rarely earns a payoff. For a reflective piece, preserve the thread of observation and reasoning without manufacturing conflict.

Assign each beat a job, its support or action, and the question it resolves or creates. Remove repetition that neither deepens understanding nor changes the situation. Plan chapters for a longer piece when they help navigation and progression. Do not merely repeat a short-form hook formula at every boundary.

## Body: make the substance usable

An explanation can introduce a point, demonstrate its application and connect it to the larger question. Make the application tangible: show the action, worked example or decision the viewer can use, then explain when it fits. A story can move through action, consequence and a changed choice. An interview uses questions and coverage goals while leaving actual answers to the participant. Give concrete examples or visible process where they carry proof; an unexplained list of impressive terms is not an explanation.

Order by comprehension and cause. For independent examples, a useful early point followed by a stronger example is one possible escalation. A “second-best first, best second” rule must not move conclusions ahead of prerequisites or hide useful information just to delay the answer. Transitions should explain why the next beat follows: a consequence, complication or next necessary step. “But” and “therefore” help only when those relationships exist. Reopen curiosity only when the next beat can fulfill it.

## Payoff: resolve what was promised

Deliver the specific answer, demonstrated result, emotional change or comic resolution established earlier. A tutorial may distribute value throughout; its final beat can show the completed result or clarify its limit. Preserve qualifications that materially change the claim. A CTA is optional. When requested, connect it to the same task: name the supplied resource or offer, how it helps the next step and the actual destination. Keep the explanation complete without taking the offer; a keyword reply needs a real fulfillment path and does not authorize sending DMs. A replay seam can be elegant only if one viewing still feels complete.

Test the ending against the opening in plain language: “We promised this; the viewer receives this.” If they differ, revise the promise or the story. Another teaser, an unearned reveal or a request to subscribe cannot close the gap.

## Performance: write speakable words

Address the intended viewer in the project's voice. Vary sentence length for natural rhythm; keep useful pauses, reactions and breathing room. Avoid universal rules about fragments, rapid cuts, word counts or punctuation. Separate exact words from emotional direction, pronunciation aliases and sound events so control tags do not reach dialogue or captions.

Read aloud when possible, or label a text-only rhythm review honestly. State the timing method: word-rate estimate, human readthrough, generated scratch or measured final recording. A word-rate estimate needs allowances for demonstrations, silence and visual reading. Keep intended timing ranges until actual media settles the timeline. Preserve continuous recordings or longer performance passages where they work; stable IDs and measured spans matter more than one file per sentence.

## Visual handoff: show the story

For each important beat, specify the opening state, meaningful action and end state, with necessary framing, on-screen text and sound. A new crop of the same still does not establish a missing action. Identify supplied footage, authentic screen captures, illustration/composition and material still to be produced. Specify actual reference files and their roles when available; a prose mention alone is not an attached reference.

Keep narration, visible dialogue, native clip sound, music and silence distinct. A speaking character may need synchronized performance; silent footage cannot supply it automatically. Production translates this intent into a shot plan and measured timeline. Preserve gaps rather than claiming that all shots or timings already exist.
