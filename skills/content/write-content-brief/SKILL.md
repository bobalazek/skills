# Write content brief

## Reuse the accepted assignment

Produce a bounded brief another writer can execute. Read the selected topic, audience, goal, destination, source material and accepted decisions; reuse the strategy and applicable voice guide instead of asking the same intake again. A brief is an assignment with evidence and acceptance conditions, beyond a bare outline. If the user requests only an outline or the finished piece, use the appropriate writing action or do that plain task when unavailable; do not insert a mandatory briefing stage.

Inspect available sources and project conventions first. Ask concise questions about consequential missing intent, audience or scope and wait before dependent decisions. Continue organizing supported material and explicit research gaps. When routine choices are delegated, propose them with their basis. Do not choose a new positioning, offer, audience or commercial term merely to fill a template.

## Make the piece executable

Define the reader's question, useful angle, supported promise and intended takeaway or action. Specify the content type and destination, relevant constraints and available voice examples. An educational answer may finish without a sales CTA. Include length or title options only when requested or useful; preserve accepted targets and explain if the supplied evidence cannot support them without repetition.

For a full assignment or an external writer, adapt [the writer assignment template](references/writer-assignment.template.md). Build the outline around reader questions, with each section's purpose, supported points and source or research requirement. Include examples, visual/media needs, links and format instructions only when they help the piece. Distinguish supplied assets from proposed production; do not imply an image or demonstration exists.

Map material claims to accessible sources and note what those sources actually establish, their freshness and any use restrictions. Separate verified facts, attributed experience, inference and open research. An unverified result, testimonial or number remains an evidence request or an excluded claim, never placeholder copy a writer could mistake for fact. Inspect supplied competitor material before claiming a gap; identify inaccessible sources without inventing their content. Keep private notes separate from public wording.

Search fields are conditional on the assignment. Use verified query evidence when supplied or researched; label unsupported keyword ideas as hypotheses. Require natural, accurate wording rather than fixed keyword positions, link quotas or presumed ranking benefits. Do not invent citations, existing URLs, traffic or search demand. Keep unknown destinations as explicit tasks, not plausible fabricated links.

## Check the handoff

Return the actual brief in the requested or existing format, with clear ready sections, blocked claims, evidence owners if known and acceptance checks. Walk it as a fresh writer: can they tell what the piece must answer, which claims they can use, what to omit, and how completion is judged? Verify cited support and consistency with accepted context; report source access or other checks not performed.

Before acceptance, obtain a separate fresh-context assessment against the original assignment, raw sources and candidate brief. Retain the returned assessment and artifact identity, resolve supported defects and recheck affected sections. Without it, label the brief unreviewed; do not substitute an author's check or impose a new human approval gate.

For an accepted brief with drafting authorized, pass it to `write-blog-post`, `write-website-copy` or `write-video-script` as appropriate, including source references, voice and exact gaps. Use `plan-content-strategy` only when the missing result is choosing work across a collection, or `plan-landing-page` for an unsettled page argument. Check availability or describe the plain action. Finish a brief-only request without writing the piece, publishing or contacting anyone; continue further work already authorized without reopening settled decisions.
