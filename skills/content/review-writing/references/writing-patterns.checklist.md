# Writing patterns to check

Use on prose whose clarity or voice needs review. Read the whole passage first. These are diagnostic questions, not evidence of who wrote it or a find-and-replace blacklist. Apply the audience's language and project style; protect accurate meaning before polishing.

## Find the actual defect

| Pattern | What to inspect | Useful repair |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Broad praise | Could the sentence describe any product while telling this reader nothing? | Name a supported capability or remove it. Ordinary labels such as "Contact" can be generic and useful. |
| Inflated significance | Does the passage announce importance without showing a consequence? | Give the established change and its effect. Leave the effect unknown when it has not been observed. |
| Borrowed authority | Is "experts agree" hiding an absent source or overstating one? | Identify the source and its actual scope, narrow the statement or flag the evidence gap. |
| Delayed subject | Does the opening spend the reader's attention announcing what it will discuss? | Start at the useful point. Keep a scene, story or question that does real work for this audience. |
| Repeated explanation | Does a sentence tell readers that the previous sentence matters, without adding information? | Remove the commentary or supply the missing causal link from known facts. |
| Manufactured revelation | Does a dramatic question, contrast or secret create stakes the content cannot support? | State the actual difference or finding. Preserve comparisons that explain a real decision. |
| Abstract action | Can the reader tell who does what? Are nouns concealing the actor or mechanism? | Use a precise subject and verb; preserve necessary domain terms and explain them where needed. |
| Decorative rhythm | Do repeated fragments, parallel clauses or forced trios add emphasis without meaning? | Cut repetition and let the material determine sentence length. Keep intentional rhythm and complete lists. |
| Terminology drift | Do new synonyms make one concept look like several? | Use a consistent name unless the distinction is meaningful. |
| Empty qualification | Does layered hedging weaken a supported point, or would removing it turn uncertainty into certainty? | Keep the qualifier that expresses the actual evidence limit; remove only the redundant layer. |
| Decorative formatting | Do headings, bold labels or bullets fragment an otherwise simple thought? | Use structure for navigation or comparison. Preserve an effective format and required document schema. |
| Unearned ending | Does the ending repeat the introduction or promise a grand future? | End at the supported conclusion, useful question or next action already earned by the piece. |
| Invented personality | Did an edit add an anecdote, opinion, deliberate error or profanity the author never supplied? | Restore the author's actual stance and voice. Natural writing does not require manufactured imperfections. |

Words such as "leverage," "innovative" or "seamless" deserve scrutiny when they hide meaning. They can have precise uses; technical, quoted and legal text needs context. Do not substitute a plainer but inaccurate term. Punctuation choices and list length follow the requested style, not an assumed AI detector.

## Examples and non-examples

These examples are synthetic. Their facts are supplied here solely to demonstrate the editing decision.

- Known fact: a report now includes a CSV export. "Our groundbreaking update transforms how teams work" becomes "You can now export the report as a CSV." No claim about time saved follows from that fact.
- Known fact: a team observed a failure in one test. Keep "This may affect other accounts; we have tested one." Removing "may" would misstate the evidence.
- "The import felt like wrestling a filing cabinet" can stay when it is the author's account and suits the audience. Vivid language is not automatically filler.
- "Read, write and admin" can be the complete set of supported roles. Do not change its length to avoid a three-item list.

## Recheck the whole passage

Read the original and candidate side by side. Preserve factual scope, qualifications, useful detail and authorial intent. Check that the continuation fulfills the opening, that headings describe their sections and that repetition was removed without flattening the voice. A passage can need no edits. Return exact findings or the requested revision, never an authorship verdict.
