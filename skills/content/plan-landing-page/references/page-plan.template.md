# Page content plan

Use for a complete page or a handoff. Reuse the project's brief and omit fields already covered by authoritative links.

## Context

- Page and revision; new page or proposed change to an existing one.
- Visitor task, audience, arrival source/promise and current alternative; evidence or assumption for each consequential choice.
- Verified offer, limits, central message and public proof sources.
- Main action and destination; confirmed commitment, cost and what happens next. Other valid visitor paths to preserve.
- Existing voice, terminology, locale and content constraints.

## Existing-page inventory

For a redesign, inspect the current page and affected destinations before proposing a new sequence. Reuse any current content inventory. A visual refresh alone does not establish that the content or offer should change.

| Existing section or content key | Need, useful content and destination | Evidence or constraint | Proposed treatment and reason |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Stable identifier | What it answers; real links or actions | Current facts, public-use rights, legal wording, search/usage evidence where available | Keep, edit, combine, move or remove; what happens to its useful content |

Account for navigation, contact/help paths, conditions and returning visitors as well as the primary conversion path. Label missing usage evidence; absence of analytics does not prove that a section is unused. Record a moved answer's new location. Proposed route retirement or redirects need a separate implementation decision; do not silently break a destination through a content plan.

## Choose useful sections

Use these as questions to resolve, not a page recipe. One section can answer several questions, and a short page may need only an explanation and an action. Order information by the visitor's actual uncertainty and prerequisites. Essential conditions belong beside the affected claim or action even when they also appear later.

| Visitor need and possible section | What to say or show | When useful; when to combine or omit | Intended purpose and check |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| "What is this?" Intro or opening | The actual offer, who it serves and the task or outcome it supports; match the arrival promise | Useful when the purpose is unclear on entry; a direct task screen may already establish it | Orientation: can the visitor explain what is offered without guessing? |
| "Does it fit my situation?" Use cases, scope or eligibility | Supported cases, prerequisites and material exclusions; use real audience language | Include differences that change suitability; omit invented personas or repeated benefit cards | Fit: can the visitor identify a relevant case and a meaningful limit? |
| "What does it do for me?" Capabilities or benefits | Connect an actual capability to a concrete consequence; distinguish demonstrated behavior from hoped-for results | Useful when feature names hide their value; combine with a demonstration when that answers it better | Understanding: can the visitor connect the capability to their task? |
| "How does it work?" Demonstration or process | Real inputs, steps, output and setup obligations; label proposed visuals and unavailable evidence | Useful when the mechanism or effort is uncertain; omit steps that add no information | Understanding and effort: can the visitor describe what they and the product must do? |
| "Why believe this?" Evidence beside a claim, example or case | An inspected demonstration, attributable evidence or approved customer account, with relevant limits | Place proof where a claim needs it; omit unsupported logos, testimonials and decorative statistics | Confidence: does the evidence support this exact claim, and can the visitor distinguish capability from an outcome claim? |
| "Which option and what commitment?" Comparison, scope or pricing | Confirmed differences, cost, eligibility and obligations; explain uncertainty when a quote is required | Include only decision-relevant options; do not invent prices or a comparison to fill a layout | Choice: can the visitor compare relevant options and recognize the commitment? |
| "What is stopping me?" Answer near the concern or a focused FAQ | A documented objection or practical question and its supported answer | Prefer an answer beside the decision it affects; a separate FAQ needs real remaining questions | Uncertainty: is the blocker answered where it arises, with important conditions still visible? |
| "What happens next?" Action and nearby explanation | The real destination, submission consequence, required information and confirmed next step | Include actions that serve a valid visitor path; repeat only when the location helps | Commitment: can the visitor predict the action's result and choose a suitable alternative? |

These purposes are design hypotheses, not psychological laws or promised conversion effects. An expert walkthrough checks consistency; participant tasks can check understanding, and an appropriately designed experiment is needed for performance claims. Record the actual observation separately from the intended effect.

## Content sequence and design handoff

| Order / section | Visitor question and purpose | Required answer or claim | Evidence and public-use status | Useful next action | Reason and check |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Existing or proposed section ID | What the visitor needs to understand or decide | Meaning to convey; final prose can follow | Accepted source, observable demonstration or exact gap | Real destination/action, or none | Why here, what uncertainty it addresses and how to check it |

For each selected section, give the writer a concrete content requirement and the designer the necessary content shape, such as a workflow example, comparison or demonstration. Include available assets, content length/variation constraints and missing inputs when they affect the design. Section IDs and action destinations should survive the copy and wireframe handoffs. Screen geometry and styling remain design decisions.

For example, a synthetic calendar product with a demonstrated conflict preview can answer "Will this replace my calendar?" by explaining the confirmed preview workflow. It cannot claim fewer missed meetings without outcome evidence. A short demonstration may be enough; an invented testimonial is not a substitute.

## Handoff

Record meaningful changes and omissions with reasons, unresolved facts/choices and their owners, and the paths checked. Link the independent assessment to the plan revision. Name the next missing output without repeating its procedure. Keep plan approval, copy readiness, rendered verification and measured outcomes distinct.

## Guidance behind the checks

- [GOV.UK user needs](https://www.gov.uk/service-manual/user-research/start-by-learning-user-needs) grounds content in actual tasks and constraints. Internal assumptions need evidence.
- [Google Ads landing-page guidance](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/6238826?hl=en) supports matching the arrival promise and making needed information and actions easy to find. It does not establish a universal section sequence or conversion improvement.
- [W3C link purpose](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/link-purpose-in-context.html) supports understandable destinations. A content plan alone cannot establish rendered accessibility.

The section choices above are a practical synthesis of the page's content decisions, not a sequence prescribed by these sources. Recheck current guidance when its behavior matters to the task.
