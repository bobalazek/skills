# Plan landing page

## Establish the page's job

Produce a content plan connecting the visitor's arrival expectations to an informed next action. Reuse accepted product research, offer details, existing page content and project conventions. Record their sources and freshness. A new product needs honest capability evidence; an existing page needs inspection of its current promise, destinations and useful content before replacement.

Reuse the applicable voice guide, commonly `docs/voice.md`, when proposing language. Use `define-writing-voice` for a separately requested reusable profile; a page's content structure does not require one. Pass available voice examples and unsettled preferences into the copy handoff.

Ask only consequential missing questions. Who arrives, from where, and what did the ad, search result or shared link promise? What are they trying to do or decide? What do they already know, and what alternative do they use? Which product capabilities and limits matter to that decision? What happens after the main action, including any confirmed cost, commitment or eligibility?

Use existing feedback for audience language and objections. Label an inferred need as a hypothesis. Research a disputed factual premise with current primary evidence; use `validate-product-idea` when the requested result is evidence for investing in the product itself. Planning a page cannot validate demand.

## Connect promises to evidence

Select a central supported message and the useful next action. Check the arrival promise, page claim and destination together. An advertised free trial cannot lead to mandatory paid onboarding without resolving that conflict; do not invent an offer to make the sequence fit. Continue unaffected planning while naming the exact fact or human decision needed.

Map material claims to observed capabilities, documented terms or approved public evidence. Keep proposed positioning distinct from established differentiation. Do not invent testimonials, customer counts, guarantees, scarcity or outcome improvements. Preserve private research and customer identities; public use of evidence needs appropriate rights. A genuine demonstration can explain a capability when customer proof is unavailable.

## Arrange the content

Order sections around visitor questions: what it does, whether it fits, how it works, why a claim is credible, and what taking the next step involves. Include only questions relevant to this page; do not impose a fixed section count, FAQ, comparison table or testimonial block. Put essential meaning and conditions where visitors need them rather than hiding them behind a curiosity hook.

A campaign destination may support one narrow task; a homepage can serve several audiences and returning users. Preserve useful navigation and distinct paths. Do not apply universal rules about removing navigation, placing every action above the fold or reaching a word count. Screen geometry belongs to wireframing; this output defines content needs and order.

For a whole page, redesign or design handoff, use [the content inventory, section decisions and plan](references/page-plan.template.md). Explain what each useful section must answer, its supported content and intended decision purpose; identify how that purpose could be checked. Preserve useful existing content and destinations before proposing replacements. Adapt the existing brief rather than duplicating it. A bounded section request needs only its changed question, message, evidence and next action. Keep missing facts separate from accepted content.

## Evaluate the plan

Keep proposed messages concrete and supported, without generic praise or invented proof. If polishing audience-facing wording is part of this request, use `review-writing` when available for its prose checks; otherwise remove filler without changing meaning or certainty. A content plan does not need a separate copy-editing stage when no finished copy is requested.

Walk each intended arrival through the proposed content to its actual destination. Check whether the offer, audience fit, material conditions and action can be understood, and whether every section answers a relevant question. Compare an existing page with the proposed change and explain what is retained, removed or still uncertain. A walkthrough is an expert check, not evidence of user comprehension or conversion lift.

Before acceptance, have a separate agent in fresh context challenge the raw request, product sources, candidate plan and destination evidence without the author's conversation or preferred answer. Retain its returned reviewer/session identity, assessed revision, findings and coverage. Resolve defects and independently recheck affected claims or paths. Without that assessment, label the plan unreviewed. Required human approval remains separate.

Return the plan, consequential choices, proof gaps and observed checks at the requested depth. Keep it in the existing authorized project location; drafting does not authorize publishing, buying traffic or contacting customers. Include safe before/after evidence and the returned assessment when an authorized PR contains the work.

## Next steps

Use `write-website-copy` for wording from the accepted plan, `map-user-flows` for unresolved journeys or navigation, `create-wireframes` for screen structure, or `design-interface` when structure already suffices and visual detail is next. Copy and layout can develop together once their shared message, content needs and action are accepted; reconcile actual text fit before delivery.

Use `test-usability` for participant understanding, `analyze-product-usage` for existing website observations, or `plan-product-experiment` when a controlled comparison is the requested next result. Carry the accepted plan, sources, revision and exact gap; check availability or describe the plain action. Finish a planning-only request, or continue ready work already authorized.
